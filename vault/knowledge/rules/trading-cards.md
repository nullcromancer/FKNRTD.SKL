# Trading card recognition

- Establish card boundaries from visible card edges. A nine-pocket page has up to nine independent slots; count occupied slots individually and do not infer cards from empty sleeves, reflections, or backing material.
- Inspect every occupied region separately. Record readable player/character, team, set/year, card number, manufacturer, language, parallel/foil, autograph, grading, and other visible identifiers. Keep unknown fields null.
- Do not identify a card by player alone when multiple years, sets, numbers, or variants fit. Use an authoritative checklist or specialist set database to corroborate the printed number and design.
- Distinguish the product/card identity from the individual physical copy. Two matching cards in a photo are separate items. Associate photos only when evidence shows the exact same physical card (wear, centering, sleeve marks, or other stable traits).
- Condition observations are not professional grades. Note visible corners, edges, surface, centering, sleeve/glare limits, and avoid grading through a sleeve.
- For valuation, search Google and eBay first; favor completed/sold listings with matching set, number, variant, grade, and condition. Clearly label dealer, asking, retail, and sold bases. Do not treat unmatched active asks as realized prices.
