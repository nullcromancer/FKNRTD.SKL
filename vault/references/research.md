# Identification and valuation policy

## Identity

1. Read logos, labels, stamps, model numbers and packaging. Validate OCR guesses against product line catalogs.
2. Compare shape, dimensions, materials, connectors, controls, hardware, color and era against reference imagery. Distinguishing details matter more than broad resemblance.
3. Use the narrowest supported label:
   - `exact`: make/model/meaningful variant established by distinctive features or readable marks.
   - `likely`: brand/product family credible but model/variant unresolved.
   - `generic`: item function/category clear, specific brand or model unsupported.
   - `unknown`: insufficient evidence even for a useful category.
4. Where lookalikes have different values, describe alternatives in Notes; price only the supported category or leave blank.

## Identification confidence

Confidence is the probability the **stated identity at its stated specificity** is correct, *not* the reliability of valuation.

| Confidence | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 95–100% | Near-certain exact identification | Readable model label and matching design |
| 80–94% | Strong match | Several independent distinctive features |
| 60–79% | Probable model/family | Design matches, but key label/variant absent |
| 40–59% | Weak inference | Multiple lookalikes or partial view |
| 0–39% | Unknown/speculative | Occluded item or contradictory evidence |

A generic name can receive high confidence when the generic classification is truly clear. Do not assign high confidence to a specific model based only on a loosely similar web photo.

## Market value

Default: plausible US secondhand resale price for a *single* item in observed or unknown condition on research date, excluding shipping and tax. Do not equate insurance replacement value with resale.

Evidence hierarchy:

1. Recent actual **sold/completed** transactions for same model/variant and comparable condition; verify sold status.
2. Reputable dealer historical sales and auction results, with fees noted.
3. Active **asking** listings for equivalent goods, labeled as asking.
4. **Retail** replacement prices as last resort, labeled retail.

Find two or more comparable transactions where practical. Favor recent 12-month sales for liquid goods; rare vintage items can use older evidence with caveat. Reject bundles versus singles, parts versus complete, different editions and misleading accessories. Convert other currencies with a sourced dated rate. Exclude obvious outliers, explain large spreads and apply known condition differences.

With enough sold prices, a median is a useful central estimate; provide a low/high plausible range. With thin evidence, widen the range and label its basis. One asking price never justifies an exact sold-value claim. Use a **direct, inspected evidence page** URL rather than a search landing page. If no defensible evidence exists, enter null amounts, `unavailable` for price basis, and explain.

Flag items whose worth depends on authentication or grading; the photo alone cannot establish provenance, maker attribution, rarity or authenticity.
